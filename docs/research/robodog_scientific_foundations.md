# RoboDog scientific foundations: primary-source notes

This note records the primary sources used to revise `docs/robodog_architecture.tex` into a paper-style technical report. Claims below are limited to official documentation, original papers, project repositories, or local RoboDog implementation files.

## Locomotion, gait and task-space control

- Raibert's legged locomotion work separates balance into body attitude, forward velocity and hopping/height regulation. The same design idea appears in RoboDog as a foot-placement term for velocity, a body-wrench balance layer for height/attitude, and joint impedance underneath it. Source: Raibert, *Legged Robots That Balance*, MIT Press, 1986, https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262681193/legged-robots-that-balance/; CMU RI report summary, https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/dynamically-stable-legged-locomotion-second-report-to-darpa-october-11981-december-311982. Local evidence: `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/gait.py`, `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/balance.py`.
- The RoboDog phase model is a standard legged-gait schedule: a normalized cycle phase plus leg offsets determines stance/swing contact state; duty factor is the stance fraction. Local evidence: `GAIT_OFFSETS` and `DEFAULT_DUTY` in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/gait.py`, and tuned values in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/config/gaits.yaml`.
- Operational-space force control maps desired task-space forces into joint torques through the Jacobian transpose. RoboDog uses the same mathematical form at the foot, with the sign convention defined as environment-on-foot ground reaction and actuator torque `-J^T f`. Source: Khatib, "The Operational Space Formulation in Robot Manipulator Control," 1985, https://cs.stanford.edu/group/manips/publications/pdfs/Khatib_1985_ISIR.pdf. Local evidence: `torque_for_force()` and `gravity_torque()` in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/kinematics.py`.
- Impedance control treats motion control as setting a dynamic relation between motion error and force/torque. RoboDog expresses this in the RS06 native command as desired position, velocity, stiffness, damping and feed-forward torque. Source: Hogan, "Impedance Control: An Approach to Manipulation: Part I--Theory," 1985, https://doi.org/10.1115/1.3140702. Local evidence: `JointCommand` in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_hardware/robodog_hardware/types.py` and command assembly in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/control_node.py`.
- Minimum-jerk trajectories use the polynomial `10s^3 - 15s^4 + 6s^5` to produce zero endpoint velocity and acceleration. RoboDog uses this for named-pose moves so pose transitions do not begin with a torque step. Local evidence: `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/trajectory.py`.
- Raibert et al. classify diagonal, lateral, and fore/hind leg-pair timing as trot, pace, and bound and show a controller organized around height, forward velocity, and attitude. Source: M. H. Raibert et al., “Dynamically Stable Legged Locomotion,” *IEEE Journal of Robotics and Automation*, 2(2), 70–82, 1986, https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/publications/dynamically-stable-legged-locomotion/.
- MIT Cheetah 3 is useful context for high-bandwidth proprioceptive actuation and robust dynamic quadruped design, while whole-body/MPC literature adds an optimized force-planning layer that RoboDog does not implement. Sources: G. Bledt et al., “MIT Cheetah 3: Design and Control of a Robust, Dynamic Quadruped Robot,” IROS 2018, DOI `10.1109/IROS.2018.8593885`; D. Kim et al., “Highly Dynamic Quadruped Locomotion via Whole-Body Impulse Control and Model Predictive Control,” 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06586.

## ROS 2 architecture and frames

- A ROS 2 node is the unit of computation in the graph. Nodes communicate through typed topics, services, actions and parameters. Source: ROS 2 documentation source, `About-Nodes.rst`, https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/rolling/source/ROS-Framework/About-Nodes.rst.
- ROS 2 topics are strongly typed publish/subscribe streams and are appropriate for continuous state and sensor data. Source: ROS 2 documentation source, `About-Topics.rst`, https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/rolling/source/ROS-Framework/interfaces/About-Topics.rst.
- ROS 2 QoS profiles combine history, depth, reliability and durability policies. Sensor data commonly uses best effort to prefer timely readings; transient local durability provides late-joiner behavior when both publisher and subscriber are compatible. Source: ROS 2 documentation source, `About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.rst`, https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/rolling/source/ROS-Framework/interfaces/topics/About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.rst.
- `robot_state_publisher` consumes URDF plus `sensor_msgs/msg/JointState` and publishes movable transforms on `/tf` and fixed transforms on `/tf_static`. Source: upstream README, https://github.com/ros/robot_state_publisher. Local evidence: bringup launch in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_bringup/launch/robot.launch.py`.
- RoboDog uses REP-103 body-frame conventions: x forward, y left, z up. Source: REP-103 source, https://github.com/ros-infrastructure/rep/blob/master/rep-0103.rst. Local evidence: `conventions` in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_description/config/robot_parameters.yaml`.
- RoboDog uses the REP-105 mobile-frame split: control publishes `odom -> base_link` and SLAM publishes `map -> odom`. Source: REP-105 source, https://github.com/ros-infrastructure/rep/blob/master/rep-0105.rst. Local evidence: `ros2_ws/src/robodog_perception/launch/mapping.launch.py` and `ros2_ws/src/robodog_control/robodog_control/control_node.py`.

## Simulation, torque reporting and RS06 limits

- MuJoCo maps actuator outputs into generalized joint force through `mjData.qfrc_actuator`. This is the correct field for applied actuator generalized force after actuator/transmission processing, not a raw requested command. Source: MuJoCo computation docs, https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/. Local evidence: sampling in `tools/torque_report.py` and `tools/go2_benchmark.py`.
- MuJoCo represents multijoint dynamics in generalized coordinates and was designed for model-based control with contact and constraint dynamics. Source: E. Todorov, T. Erez, and Y. Tassa, “MuJoCo: A Physics Engine for Model-Based Control,” IROS 2012, DOI `10.1109/IROS.2012.6386109`, https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~todorov/papers/TodorovIROS12.pdf.
- RMS torque is an energy/load summary over a window and must be interpreted with peaks, overload exposure and clipping. RoboDog records both RMS and peak per motor and keeps startup peaks in the peak/exposure statistics while excluding the first five seconds from the 100 s RMS window. Local evidence: `docs/rs06_28kg_1ms_100s.md`, `docs/rs06_28kg_1ms_100s.json`, and tests in `tools/test_torque_report.py`.
- RS06 performance values in the architecture document come from the September 17, 2026 RS series product specification, with the July RS06 manual retained for CAN/protocol details. The published 8 N m continuous stall point is distinct from the 11 N m rotating rating with a heatsink. Source audit: `docs/rs06_datasheet_audit.md`; live config: `ros2_ws/src/robodog_description/config/robstride06.yaml`.
- The external knee belt is modeled as ratio 2 and efficiency 0.95, so motor torque is joint torque divided by 1.9 and motor speed is twice knee speed. Local evidence: `transmissions.kfe` in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_description/config/robstride06.yaml` and `transmission_arrays()` in `ros2_ws/src/robodog_hardware/robodog_hardware/transmission.py`.

## SLAM, OctoMap and point clouds

- RTAB-Map is a graph-based appearance/visual SLAM system for large-scale and long-term online operation. It supports RGB-D, stereo, and lidar configurations and maintains a database/graph of observations. Source: M. Labbé and F. Michaud, “RTAB-Map as an Open-Source Lidar and Visual SLAM Library for Large-Scale and Long-Term Online Operation,” *Journal of Field Robotics* 36(2), 416–446, 2019, DOI `10.1002/rob.21831`, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rob.21831; ROS wrapper: https://github.com/introlab/rtabmap_ros. Local evidence: `ros2_ws/src/robodog_perception/launch/mapping.launch.py`.
- OctoMap is an octree-based probabilistic 3D occupancy mapping framework that models occupied, free and unknown space. Source: Hornung et al., "OctoMap: an efficient probabilistic 3D mapping framework based on octrees," Autonomous Robots, 2013, https://octomap.github.io/ and https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-012-9321-0.
- `sensor_msgs/msg/PointCloud2` stores an N-dimensional point collection as a binary blob with layout described by fields, and may be organized as a 2D image-like cloud from depth sensors. Source: ROS 2 message definition, https://github.com/ros2/common_interfaces/blob/rolling/sensor_msgs/msg/PointCloud2.msg.
- RoboDog saves room scans as a graph-corrected assembled PCD, full colored OctoMap, RTAB-Map database backup and manifest. Local evidence: `ros2_ws/src/robodog_perception/robodog_perception/map_export.py`, `ros2_ws/src/robodog_msgs/srv/SaveMap.srv`, and `docs/mapping.md`.

## Evidence limits established by the research pass

- Primary sources justify the mathematical forms and middleware semantics; they do not validate RoboDog’s gains, geometry, actuator cooling, camera calibration, or gait performance.
- The current mapping test uses MuJoCo ground-truth odometry. It validates synchronization, TF ownership, map growth, persistence, reload, and export, but not visual-odometry or loop-closure accuracy on a physical robot.
- The current 28 kg gait data are deterministic single simulation runs. A stable body pose does not by itself satisfy velocity tracking or actuator margin; RMS must be read with peak torque, overload time, speed at peak, and safety-limiter intervention.
- The Go2 benchmark uses the official model with RoboDog’s shared controller and measurement method. It is not evidence about Unitree’s factory controller.
